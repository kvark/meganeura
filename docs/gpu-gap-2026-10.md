# Intel attention backward — October 5–6, 2026

Batching the scalar attention-gradient reductions cuts Whisper-tiny training
time by 35.5% on Arc B570 and SmolLM2-135M training time by 12.5%. These are
current-code engineering measurements, separate from the frozen P3HPC cohort.
The change does not address the Intel inference gap.

## What the profiles showed

The starting revision was `6288f88`. Strict-f32 SmolVLA spent about 60% of its
instrumented GPU time in matrix operations and 21% in attention backward.
Whisper-tiny spent 73% in attention backward. Its four `FlashGradKV` dispatches
accounted for 62.10 ms and its four `FlashGradQ` dispatches for 18.28 ms.

The multi-lane backward kernels staged and reduced one query/key row at a
time. Each row incurred a staging barrier, a shared-memory reduction tree,
and a barrier before reusing that storage. They repeated this work across
Whisper's 1,500 positions. Forward attention already batched score reductions.

## Change

The grouped dQ and dK/dV kernels now stage up to four rows and reduce their
partial dot products together. A reduction-tree barrier covers the entire
tile. dK/dV flattens the query-position/head sequence within each KV head so
that accumulation retains its original order. Tail rows are zero-filled and
masked; causal and sliding-window bounds still apply per query/key pair.

Inputs, partial sums and outputs remain f32. The tile shrinks for wider heads
to target a 16 KiB staging budget; tested head widths through 1,024 fit that
limit. The single-lane and one-query kernels keep their existing algorithms.
The kernel change adds no configuration switches or device-name heuristics
and leaves graph search, submission scheduling, precision policy and
qualification unchanged.

The updated Whisper profile attributes 23.04 ms to dK/dV and 15.28 ms to dQ.
Attention backward remains the largest family, at 56% of instrumented GPU
time. Timestamped passes perturb execution: these intervals explain the
location of the improvement, while the table below uses ordinary synchronized
host-wall timings. They do not measure a separate barrier-overhead fraction.

## Repeated measurements

Median of three fresh-process medians, milliseconds per forward + loss +
backward step; no optimizer update. Every process warms for at least two
seconds and retains 20 synchronized samples. Baseline/candidate process order
alternates. Both binaries load the same selected plan and use one submission
per step. No builds or other benchmark processes overlap a measurement.

| GPU | Workload | Baseline | Batched reductions | Speedup |
|---|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Arc B570 | Whisper-tiny | 95.359 | 61.480 | 1.551× |
| Arc B570 | SmolLM2-135M | 88.897 | 77.777 | 1.143× |
| Arc B570 | SmolVLA | 19.601 | 19.635 | 0.998× |
| RTX 5070 | Whisper-tiny | 32.739 | 28.884 | 1.133× |
| RTX 5070 | SmolLM2-135M | 37.482 | 36.065 | 1.039× |
| RTX 5070 | SmolVLA | 10.944 | 10.953 | 0.999× |

The Intel Whisper process medians were 94.185–95.596 ms before and
61.264–61.688 ms after. SmolLM2's were 88.722–88.929 and 77.747–77.792 ms.
SmolVLA's default short-sequence backward uses the unchanged one-query kernels;
its small difference is noise. Forward hashes match across each process pair.
There is no inference-kernel change, so apparent small inference differences
are not counted as gains.

The saved plans were selected on Intel and also used for the NVIDIA checks.
This holds the program fixed when testing the kernel change; it is not a new
comparison of independently tuned frameworks. No PyTorch baseline was rerun.
Inputs and objectives come from the paper's Inferena runner: SmolLM2 uses the
pinned pretrained 135M weights, and the other models use deterministic
parameters. Input uploads and output/gradient readbacks are outside timing.

Environment: i5-12400F, Linux `7.0.0-34-generic`, Arc B570 with Mesa
`26.0.3-1ubuntu1`, and RTX 5070 with driver `595.91.07`. Processes use CPU
affinity `0,2,4,6,8,10`. Existing driver caches are retained. Clock and power
settings were not changed. The Arc remains on the host's PCIe x1 link.

## Other experiments

Before changing the shaders, varying the existing backward elements-per-thread
cap gave useful gains on a fixed selected plan. A cap of 16 changed Whisper
from 95.226 to 69.799 ms; a cap of 8 changed SmolVLA from 19.725 to 17.088 ms.
With the batched dK/dV prototype, cap 4 gave SmolVLA 16.258 versus its
19.688 ms control. Each comparison checked every output and parameter-gradient
element before and after timing, using the existing fixed `1e-6 + 1e-4 * scale`
maximum/RMS bounds. These are exploratory paired runs, not the repeated
default-path results above. The best cap changed after batching: smaller is
not uniformly better.

Increasing the inherited weight-gradient K stage from 8 to 16 or 32 did not
help either model. It made SmolVLA slower by about 5–6%. Unrolling every scalar
matmul K stage also gave no useful gain. Those changes were discarded.

At the October 5 measured revision, backward layout selection remained an
opportunity. That search varied forward-attention layouts but did not
independently search the two backward layouts. Adding choices required
checking coverage and preparation cost: the 60-second SmolVLA training
searches visited only six complete plans.
The fixed-plan experiments do not establish that simply adding candidates
would select the best combination within that budget.

The October 6 follow-up implements independent dQ and dK/dV layout exploration
through egglog; see [compiler search](compiler-search.md). The measurements
above predate that search change and do not quantify its effect.

For inference, matrix efficiency remains the main lead in SmolVLA, and forward
attention remains substantial in Whisper. Rectangular cooperative-matrix
integration is still separate work for accelerated arithmetic; it cannot
explain or fix the strict-f32 gap by itself.

## Validation and reproduction

The shader-generation checks validate WGSL/SPIR-V and the staging bound.
The attention oracle checks forward/backward values against the independent
f64 interpreter, including non-power-of-two heads, grouped heads, causal and
window masks, and both tile tails, on Intel and NVIDIA. Full-model layout
experiments use all output and gradient elements; repeated cross-binary runs
also compare sampled outputs, loss and every parameter's gradient norm.

The October 5 implementation passed all 337 library tests
(five ignored) on Intel and all 18 attention-oracle tests on each GPU. No
tests failed. All 36 processes in the repeated comparisons completed and
passed their cross-binary checks.

Experiment sources and the dependency lock are on the local branch
`experiment/intel-optimization-2026-10-05`. The runner started from Inferena
`7b8fcb72`, with Blade aligned to Meganeura's `e349cddf` dependency. Control
source is `964e045`; the measured combined-kernel source is `7933d29`.
Subsequent changes remove unused template fragments and extend validation.
Local tags `experiment/intel-attention-2026-10-05-control`,
`experiment/intel-attention-2026-10-05-kv` and
`experiment/intel-attention-2026-10-05` retain the control, dK/dV-only prototype
and combined-kernel measured sources, respectively.

Build each revision's `bench/intel-study/Cargo.toml` and keep its binary.
The study runner's `INTEL_SAVE_PLAN_DIR` writes selected plans, and
`INTEL_LOAD_PLAN_DIR` replays them with one submission. This is controlled
experiment plumbing, not a supported persistent cache for `build_measured`.
The scripts, plans and recorded options must stay together.

```sh
MEGANEURA_DEVICE_ID=57868 INFERENA_STRICT=1 \
  INTEL_SAVE_PLAN_DIR=/path/to/plans /path/to/control Whisper-tiny

python3 bench/intel-study/paired.py \
  --control /path/to/control --candidate /path/to/candidate \
  --plans /path/to/plans --output /path/to/new-results \
  --device 57868 --models Whisper-tiny SmolVLA SmolLM2-135M
```

Generate a plan for each model before the paired run. Receipts, profiles,
saved plans and binary hashes are retained outside Git under
`target/intel-20261005/` in the working checkout. The `paired-*` directories
contain all process samples and summaries. Presentation and paper results
continue to use their original frozen cohort.

## Backward layout search — October 6

Rebased the kernels onto `origin/main` at `b684ffd`, then added independent
dQ and dK/dV EPT choices through the existing egglog extractor. The compiler
records the choice in each scalar flash dispatch, and the runtime includes
it in the pipeline key. Shape guards exclude layouts that would require an
unsupported wide-head scalar fallback. The ordinary incumbent and numerical
qualification remain in place.

The first search ordering put backward-only alternatives before the matrix
forms. Under SmolVLA's 60-second budget, both arms visited only six complete
programs. That ordering displaced the control's useful matrix schedule and
regressed held-out training from 19.493 to 23.180 ms. The final frontier puts
a joint matrix/backward candidate early, then visits the independent axes
and further combinations. This is coverage ordering, not an estimated cost
claim or a guarantee that a larger search space always helps.

One fresh Intel process per arm, strict f32, identical model inputs and
initialization, 16 forms, 64-program bound, and a soft 60-second training
search budget:

| Search | Held-out training (ms) | Search preparation (s) | Whole search (s) |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Backward layouts disabled | 19.493 | 0.315 | 63.003 |
| Backward-only prefix, discarded | 23.180 | 0.420 | 63.222 |
| Joint choices early | 16.103 | 0.416 | 62.682 |

The final plan selects cap 4 for all 16 dQ and 16 dK/dV sites alongside the
extracted matrix schedule. Its training time is 17.4% below the control in
this single engineering comparison. The deadline includes qualification and
cannot interrupt in-flight work. All three arms use a separate two-second
warmup and 20 synchronized held-out steps, no optimizer update. Inference
sessions use ordinary construction here; their timings are not an inference
search comparison. Processes run sequentially on the same Intel environment
and CPU affinity as October 5, with no overlapping builds or GPU tests.

Each arm passed 22 full-model qualification calls, checking the loss and
99,848,592 gradient elements in 152 tensors against its ordinary reference
with the unchanged strict bounds. Forward hashes, output shapes, loss and
every parameter-gradient norm also agree across arms. The f64 attention
tests cover mixed layouts within one session on Intel and NVIDIA. A measured
construction test requires a qualified matrix/backward combination within
six trials; CPU checks cover independent layouts, masks and wide-head guards.
Final validation passed 354 library tests (six ignored), 11 model-parity
tests (one ignored), and 19 attention tests on each GPU. The bounded measured
construction test also passes on both devices.

Sources and the locked runner are on local branch
`experiment/backward-layout-2026-10-06`. Measured source tags are
`experiment/backward-layout-2026-10-06-control` (`753651b`),
`experiment/backward-layout-2026-10-06-candidate` (`fd7dc42`, discarded order),
and `experiment/backward-layout-2026-10-06-joint` (`b039a27`, final order).
The control uses the same rebased kernels and bypasses only derivative-layout
extraction. Build `bench/intel-study/Cargo.toml` at each tag, retain its binary,
then run each arm with:
