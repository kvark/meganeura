\begin{abstract}
Can a compact native stack span training and deployed inference across
consumer GPUs? \system{} (34.5\,KLOC of Rust, 6.1\,KLOC of WGSL;
12.8\,MiB runner) lowers typed graphs and automatic differentiation to
specialized Vulkan and Metal kernels. We compare five workloads with PyTorch
on five NVIDIA, AMD, Intel, and Apple machines under correctness-gated
strict-f32 and accelerated contracts. In strict f32, \system{} wins 12 of 20
GPU-referenced minimal-latency cells; median valid training time is
$1.8\times$ PyTorch's. On discrete AMD, four of five inference workloads are
within $1.10\times$ PyTorch and three training workloads are faster.
Forty-eight of 50 device--workload--mode cells validate; two are excluded
symmetrically after a cross-backend audit identifies a PyTorch/ROCm oracle
dispute. Profiles localize the largest gaps to convolution derivatives and
Metal attention backward. Compilation takes 0.1--2.4\,s; a Quest~3S
application physically exercises the same train-to-deploy path.
\end{abstract}

\begin{IEEEkeywords}
performance portability, GPU, Vulkan, Metal, machine learning systems,
training, correctness validation
\end{IEEEkeywords}

\section{Introduction}

Performance-portability layers are usually source-code abstractions:
Kokkos, RAJA, and SYCL promise one program that runs well on heterogeneous
hardware~\cite{edwards2014kokkos,beckingsale2019raja,khronos2020sycl}, and
this community has built methodology for testing that
promise~\cite{pennycook2016metric,pennycook2021revisiting}. Consumer and edge
devices suggest a different candidate layer that HPC rarely evaluates: the
graphics APIs. Vulkan is a cross-vendor graphics and compute API; Metal
exposes the corresponding facilities on Apple platforms
\cite{khronos2026vulkan,apple2026metal}. Both increasingly expose matrix
operations. In all five machines evaluated here, the graphics driver
provides a working GPU path; on one of them, the installed vendor ML stack
does not expose its GPU at all. What graphics APIs do not provide is the
compiler, kernel library, graph execution, and
profiling ecosystem that CUDA applications enjoy, so the portability
question is really about the layer built on top.

This paper evaluates that layer through \system{}: one compact native
compiler and runtime (34.5\,KLOC of Rust, 6.1\,KLOC of WGSL) that lowers a
typed static graph and its automatically differentiated training graph to
specialized compute kernels, submitted through Vulkan on Linux, Windows,
and Android and Metal on Apple platforms via the Blade graphics
abstraction~\cite{malyshau2026barriers}. The same graph representation,
compiler, checkpoint format, memory planner, and runtime serve both
optimizer-backed training and deployed inference; the deployed application
needs neither CUDA nor ROCm nor a Python runtime. The reference is
PyTorch~\cite{paszke2019pytorch} on each machine's best usable path, with
\code{torch.compile} where it functions --- a state-of-practice reference
that is fully labeled when it falls back to eager execution or the CPU.

This is an HPC+AI question even though the evaluated systems are consumer
machines rather than supercomputers. Heterogeneous programming models now
cross datacenter, workstation, and edge boundaries; preparation for edge
systems is therefore part of the performance-portability problem, not a
separate deployment topic. The small-scale setting also exposes two costs
often hidden on managed HPC systems: whether the compute stack can be
installed at all, and whether a compiled artifact can coexist with an
interactive graphics application.

Measuring this layer honestly requires a discipline not always present in
portability studies. ML fast paths are numerically distinct by design ---
TF32 and f16-input/f32-accumulate matrix units are not the same arithmetic
--- and training adds a backward pass whose errors do not surface in
forward outputs. A portability number for ML training that is not gated on
gradient correctness measures nothing falsifiable. Every cell here is
therefore measured under explicit arithmetic contracts and gated on
independent forward and backward validation.

We make the P3 goals explicit. The \emph{performance target} is the best
usable PyTorch path on each machine under a stated arithmetic contract, not
a fraction of theoretical peak. The \emph{portability unit} is the complete
machine and installed software stack; a CPU fallback therefore remains a
supported but fully labeled result, while an invalid numerical result does
not. A paired result also requires a usable numerical oracle; an
oracle-disputed pair is retained as a diagnostic but excluded symmetrically
from both implementations' ratios and aggregate. Shape- and
capability-specific kernels are acceptable, but model code,
checkpoint semantics, and the compiler/runtime interface must remain common
across machines. For \emph{productivity}, we report operational quantities
we can reproduce --- cold compilation, deployment closure, implementation
scale, and platform bring-up --- rather than claiming that source lines or
package bytes directly measure developer time.

The study makes four contributions:
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{A portability-layer evaluation of graphics APIs for ML
        training and inference.} Five matched workloads, five consumer
        devices spanning CUDA, ROCm (discrete and integrated), Metal, and
        a nonfunctional vendor path, all at one frozen revision pair ---
        with support availability treated as part of the result, not as
        noise to be excluded.
  \item \textbf{Mechanism-resolved results.} The compact layer wins 12 of
        20 GPU-referenced minimal-shape latency cells, consistent with its
        static execution model; four of five discrete-AMD inference cells
        are within $1.10\times$ of compiled ROCm PyTorch; per-dispatch
        profiles locate dominant costs in convolution derivatives and Metal
        attention backward; capability probing explains exactly where the
        accelerated contract engages.
  \item \textbf{Correctness gates that localize, not just detect.}
        Independent forward/backward gates under strict and accelerated
        contracts, with per-parameter gradient norms recorded in every
        artifact. A cross-backend consistency audit identifies the two
        non-comparable cells as one PyTorch/ROCm oracle dispute rather than
        attributing the pairwise mismatch to \system{}
        (Section~\ref{sec:oracle}).
  \item \textbf{Validity-disciplined aggregation.} The Pennycook metric
        with application efficiency measured against the best \emph{valid}
        time per machine, zero for an invalid or unsupported implementation,
        and symmetric omission where the designated oracle itself fails the
        cross-backend consistency check.
\end{itemize}

Sections below describe the layer (\S\ref{sec:system}), define the
protocol (\S\ref{sec:method}), report the matrix (\S\ref{sec:results}),
locate the gaps with per-dispatch profiles (\S\ref{sec:gaps}), and
quantify the productivity dimension (\S\ref{sec:productivity}). The
extended system description, memory-planner and rewrite ablations, and
compiler-engineering lessons appear in the companion technical
report~\cite{malyshau2026meganeura}; this paper is self-contained for the
portability question.

\system{} executes a typed static tensor graph. Builders and ONNX/NNEF
importers construct the graph; a deterministic greedy rewriter optimizes
it (an equality-saturation mode exists but showed no runtime advantage
over the greedy fixed point for the current rule set --- details in the
technical report); training then applies reverse-mode differentiation and
an SGD or Adam update within the same static session. A precision bit
attached to derivative regions prevents an optional f16-input
cooperative-matrix promotion from rounding small gradient operands
(Section~\ref{sec:contracts}).

Portability does not mean one untuned kernel. Code generation uses a small
set of kernel archetypes --- pointwise DAGs, workgroup reductions, matrix
products with prologues/epilogues and cooperative tiles, implicit-GEMM and
spatial convolutions with their derivatives, and tiled attention
forward/backward --- specialized per shape, device capability, and
precision policy, with pipeline variant, workgroup geometry, and padding
selected atomically. WGSL is the WebGPU shading language
\cite{w3c2026wgsl}; here it is an authoring and diagnostic boundary, not a
claim that execution occurs in a browser. Naga parses and validates the
generated source, and Blade consumes the resulting module for Vulkan or
Metal. Compilation fixes one dispatch sequence over lifetime-aliased
buffers. A step walks that plan with no graph rediscovery,
application-managed capture, or dynamic allocation, although Blade still
records and submits compute work rather than replaying a native CUDA Graph.
The pinned runtime synchronizes compiler-selected pass groups; a companion
study quantifies related barrier costs across vendors
\cite{malyshau2026barriers}.

These choices make the evaluation diagnosable rather than monolithic.
Minimal-shape wins are consistent with the fixed dispatch plan, although
the end-to-end measurements do not isolate host launch cost from small-kernel
quality (Section~\ref{sec:results}). Per-dispatch profiles directly locate
the largest deficits in convolution derivatives and Metal attention
backward (Section~\ref{sec:gaps}). Capability probing --- with scalar
fallback when cooperative tiles are absent --- determines exactly where the
accelerated contract changes the generated program: a portable permission
is not a portable mechanism (Section~\ref{sec:accel}). The compiler may
select different variants across capabilities and drivers, so cross-device
agreement is evidence, not identity by construction; that distinction
matters for the oracle audit in Section~\ref{sec:oracle}. Finally, physical
source-line counts put the layer at 34.5\,KLOC of Rust and 6.1\,KLOC of WGSL;
tests and examples add 14.8\,KLOC. This is implementation scale, not a direct
productivity metric, and it also explains the gaps: a kernel library of this
size cannot cover everything mature vendor libraries cover. The count covers
one graph compiler and one Blade/WGSL path, not a collection of vendor, CPU,
and graphics backends. Post-freeze work added persistent provenance,
intermediate readback and first-NaN/Inf attribution, same-kernel eager
evaluation, and reduced the shader inventory from 75 to 55 files;
none enters the frozen counts or results.\footnote{\url{https://github.com/kvark/meganeura/pull/137}}

\section{Methodology}
\label{sec:method}

\inferena{}, the open-source cross-framework harness, constructs matched
inputs and objectives, synchronizes timing boundaries, retains raw
samples, and records precision, environment, revision, output, and
gradient metadata for every run.\footnote{\url{https://github.com/kvark/inferena}}
The harness supplies kernels to neither engine.

\subsection{Reference and comparison scope}

PyTorch is both the numerical reference and the state-of-practice
performance target. Each runner implements the same tensor shapes,
deterministic inputs, scalar objective, and canonical parameter names; it
does not share implementation kernels. The PyTorch runner uses
\code{torch.compile} on the Linux CUDA and ROCm paths after clearing its
compiler cache. MPS remains eager because compilation is unsupported in
this harness, and the Intel \code{+xpu} build falls back to eager CPU
execution because it exposes no usable XPU. Consequently, ratios compare
the best usable path on each complete machine; they must not be read as a
cross-device ranking of the accelerators themselves. No manual CUDA Graph
capture is added to the frozen compiled Linux runs. Inductor remains free
to apply its normal backend optimizations.

\subsection{Workloads}

Five matched model families cover the workload classes a portable
on-device stack plausibly serves: SmolLM2-135M (decoder-only transformer,
128-token prefill and a stateless one-token shape; 135M parameters),
the SmolVLA robotics action expert (99.85M)~\cite{shukor2025smolvla}, a
scaled Stable Diffusion 1.x U-Net (10.93M, not checkpoint-compatible with
SD~1.5)~\cite{rombach2022ldm}, ResNet-50 at batch 4~\cite{he2016resnet},
and the Whisper-tiny encoder (8.21M)~\cite{radford2022whisper}. PyTorch
and \system{} use matching shapes, objectives, canonical parameter names,
and deterministic inputs. Three timed series are reported per cell:
\emph{inference} (full no-gradient forward; prefill for the LLM),
\emph{minimal-shape latency} (one token for the LLM; no KV cache, so no
decode claim is made), and \emph{forward--loss--backward} (no optimizer
update). \emph{Compile} covers graph construction, optimization, autodiff,
and GPU pipeline creation for all three sessions. Each series takes five
untimed warmups, except the Intel sweep, which records four; every series
retains 20 timed samples. Medians are reported and raw distributions ship
in the artifact.

\subsection{Arithmetic contracts}
\label{sec:contracts}

The strict-f32 contract disables TF32 on the PyTorch side and f16
cooperative-matrix paths on the \system{} side; persistent tensors,
accumulation, and outputs are f32 for both. The accelerated contract
permits each engine's documented fast path: TF32 for PyTorch,
f16-input/f32-accumulate matrix, convolution, and attention inputs for
\system{} --- forward only; backward matrices remain f32, because an
earlier development run showed accurate forward outputs coexisting with
22\% total-gradient error when derivative operands were rounded before
cooperative multiplication. Marking differentiated regions full precision
reduced that to 0.023\%, a figure the frozen accelerated runs reproduce on
both discrete devices where the path engages.

\subsection{Correctness gates}

PyTorch is the numerical reference. A forward result is valid when the
output shape matches, relative output L2 error is below 1\%, and symmetric
relative loss error is below 1\%. A training cell additionally requires
matching canonical parameter sets, total-gradient-norm error below 5\%,
and relative L2 error below 5\% over the vector of per-parameter gradient
norms. Forward and backward validity are independent: a backward failure
invalidates the training cell without discarding a valid inference cell.
Per-parameter gradient norms are recorded in every artifact, which is what
later allows disagreements to be \emph{localized} rather than merely detected.
The designated reference must also be a usable oracle. If its deterministic
backward record is inconsistent with the other reference backends while the
candidate reproduces their consensus across devices, the pair is labeled
oracle-disputed: raw values remain visible, but neither implementation gets a
ratio or aggregate contribution from that pair
(Section~\ref{sec:oracle}).

\subsection{Strict f32}

Table~\ref{tab:strict} reports the controlled comparison. Against
\emph{compiled} CUDA PyTorch on the RTX~5070, \system{} is faster on two
of five inference cells (diffusion U-Net $0.82\times$, ResNet-50
$0.90\times$) and three of five one-token cells; the remaining inference
gaps are $1.84$--$2.65\times$ and training runs $1.8$--$2.9\times$
behind. On the RX~7900~XT the portable stack effectively reaches the
vendor stack: four of five workloads within $1.10\times$ for inference
(SmolVLA 33\% faster) and three of five training cells outright faster
(SmolVLA $0.73\times$, diffusion U-Net $0.89\times$, Whisper
$0.90\times$), with ResNet-50 the outlier in both ($1.85\times$,
$2.41\times$). On the Radeon~780M APU, \system{} wins three of five
inference cells at $0.72\times$ and two of four comparable training cells,
with only the convolution pair beyond $1.25\times$. On the Apple~M3, inference lands at
$0.93$--$1.88\times$ and training at $1.20$--$2.84\times$ against eager
MPS --- the weakest surface, and the one that improved most from the
profile-guided pass of Section~\ref{sec:gaps}. On the Intel machine the
comparison inverts: the vendor path does not function, and the portable
stack beats the resulting CPU reference in 12 of 15 cells while being the
only functional GPU path on the device.

Minimal-shape latency deserves its own sentence: \system{} is outright
faster in 12 of 20 GPU-referenced one-token/minimal cells, most visibly on
ROCm, where PyTorch's one-token SmolLM step takes 6.65\,ms against
1.93\,ms ($3.4\times$). This small shape is especially sensitive to launch
and synchronization structure, and the fixed dispatch sequence wins despite
\code{torch.compile}; the measurement does not separate host launch cost
from small-kernel quality. Correctness margins are wide: across all 25
strict cells the worst forward relative L2 error is 0.0043\% against a 1\%
gate, and across the 24 comparable backward comparisons the worst per-parameter
gradient-norm error is 0.64\% against a 5\% gate. Several per-cell error
values reproduce to three digits across all five devices: the residual
disagreement is consistent with deterministic operation-ordering differences
between the engines rather than run noise.

\subsection{Accelerated arithmetic}
\label{sec:accel}

Table~\ref{tab:accel} shows where each contract engages, which is the
portability lesson. TF32 changes PyTorch only on NVIDIA (ResNet-50
inference gains $3.1\times$); its ROCm timings are unchanged and MPS moves
only within run variation --- the permission engages only on CUDA in this
matrix.
\system{}'s f16 cooperative path engages exactly where the driver reports
the capability: both AMD devices and NVIDIA (Whisper $1.8\times$ and
$1.4\times$ on the discrete cards; SmolLM $1.3\times$ on the APU, reaching
$0.55\times$ the compiled reference), and nowhere on the M3 or the Intel
iGPU, whose accelerated timings match strict within noise. The net effect:
the NVIDIA gap widens (ResNet-50 training $4.6\times$, where TF32
convolutions compound the conv-derivative gap), while both AMD devices keep
most non-convolution cells near parity or faster. One negative result is
worth reporting: SmolVLA
\emph{regresses} under the cooperative permission on both RADV devices
($3.05\rightarrow3.82$\,ms on the 7900~XT) --- the promotion decision
currently treats an available cooperative tile as always profitable, and
it is not; a per-shape cost model is the indicated fix.

\subsection{Cross-backend records identify an oracle-disputed reference}
\label{sec:oracle}

Whisper's forward pass on the 780M validates at 0.0016\%, but the paired
backward comparison disagrees by 17.7--17.8\% in the vector of per-parameter
gradient norms across the two arithmetic modes. This is not a failed
\system{} execution: all five \system{} runs complete. Under strict f32,
their gradient vectors agree mutually within 0.00002\%; PyTorch on CUDA,
discrete ROCm, CPU, and MPS agrees within 0.001\%; and the two clusters agree
within 0.035\%. Accelerated records preserve the separation: each cluster is
internally within 0.007\%, their cross-cluster disagreement is at most
0.042\%, and the PyTorch/ROCm 780M vector remains 16.3\% from the other
PyTorch backends. Its strict total gradient norm is also 8.15\% away from
their consensus.

We therefore treat the PyTorch 780M backward record as unusable as a
numerical oracle and classify the \emph{paired comparison}, not \system{}, as
oracle-disputed. The raw \system{} and PyTorch timings and validation
diagnostics remain in both tables and the artifact, while their ratio and
both implementations' aggregate contributions are omitted. The valid 780M
forward timings remain in every inference measure. These records do not
establish the root cause inside PyTorch, ROCm, or the driver; an independent
implementation would still be useful for that diagnosis. They do establish
that this local PyTorch result cannot serve as the oracle for attributing a
\system{} correctness failure.

\caption{Pennycook performance portability over the comparable frozen set,

For implementation $i$, workload $a$, phase $q$, and its explicit comparable
machine set $S_{a,q}$, we use application efficiency
$e_{i,a,q}(p)=t_{\mathrm{best\ valid},a,q}(p)/t_{i,a,q}(p)$ and report
\begin{equation}
  P_{i,a,q}=\begin{cases}
  |S_{a,q}|\big/\sum_{p\in S_{a,q}} 1/e_{i,a,q}(p),
      & \text{valid on every }p,\\
  0, & \text{otherwise.}
  \end{cases}
\end{equation}
This is the Pennycook metric~\cite{pennycook2016metric}: harmonic mean over
the explicit comparable set, zero for an unsupported or invalid
implementation on that set. All sets contain the five frozen machines except
$S_{\text{Whisper},\text{F+L+B}}$, which excludes the one pair for which no
usable oracle exists and therefore contains four. ``Best'' here
means the better valid observation from the two evaluated implementations,
not a claim of globally best-known hardware efficiency. The final
row of Table~\ref{tab:pp} is an arithmetic summary across five independent
workload scores, not a new aggregate over applications.

The exclusion is symmetric: the Whisper training score is 0.50 for
\system{} and 0.97 for PyTorch over the same four machines. Because the
oracle-consistency rule was applied after inspecting the frozen records, we
also report its sensitivity. Mechanically assigning the pairwise mismatch to
\system{}, as an earlier draft did, would lower its mean training score
from 0.62 to 0.52; PyTorch's rounded mean would remain 0.93. No disputed
timing is used in either score. For training the incumbent is clearly more
portable (mean 0.93 versus 0.62) --- vendor training libraries are good
wherever they run; for full-shape inference the two are close (0.81 versus
0.75); for minimal-shape latency the portable stack is the more
performance-portable system (0.83 versus 0.73). The result is consistent with
the static plan reducing sensitivity to backend launch and synchronization
costs, although the end-to-end measurements also include kernel quality.

\section{Edge Deployment Beyond the Matrix}
\label{sec:edge}

The matched matrix contains no Android reference, so Android contributes no
performance-portability score. We nevertheless use a physical deployment to
test whether the same source stack closes over an edge application rather
than only benchmark runners. DinoVision reconstructs passthrough imagery
from intermediate DINOv3 features~\cite{simeoni2025dinov3}. \system{} trains
a 2.01-million-parameter decoder on a host GPU, saves its parameters, and
loads them into a joined batch-one encoder/decoder graph on a Quest~3S. The
deployed 2.359-GMAC graph runs through the same graph representation,
compiler, memory planner, checkpoint format, and Blade runtime. Two
asynchronous eye sessions share Blade's Vulkan context and queue with the
OpenXR renderer.

The deployment has an independent host/device gate. For one fixed public
RGB frame and identical weights, preprocessed patches are bit-exact; Quest
encoder output has relative $L_2=0.001059$ and the final reconstruction has
relative $L_2=0.000457$ against the host. An isolated synchronized run takes
96.78\,ms as one submission chunk. Splitting the ordered dispatch plan into
two chunks costs 4.1\% in isolation, but under a 90-second live stereo sweep
it raises application render submissions by 18.8\%, raises the lower-eye
update rate by 8.7\%, and reduces median worker latency by 20.2\%. Larger
chunk counts regress non-monotonically. The lesson is not that two chunks
are universally optimal; it is that graphics/compute co-tenancy exposes a
scheduling dimension that an inference-only benchmark misses, and the
shared graphics stack can expose that control without a second GPU runtime.

This case study establishes deployment closure and queue co-tenancy, not
on-device training or speedup over PyTorch on Android. Its weights, raw
samples, manifests, and full-resolution media are available in the public
\href{https://huggingface.co/mad-bot/dinovision}{DinoVision evidence
archive}; its \href{https://github.com/kvark/dinovision}{source code} is
public as well.

\section{Locating the Gaps}
\label{sec:gaps}

The largest valid gaps are ResNet-50 training on NVIDIA ($4.6\times$
accelerated, $2.2\times$ strict), SmolLM training on NVIDIA ($4.0\times$
accelerated), and Whisper training on the M3 ($2.8\times$). For the first
and third we captured per-dispatch hardware-timestamp profiles; both were
taken at the immediately preceding revision, a disclosure that carries
different weight in each case.

The NVIDIA ResNet-50 profile is directly comparable to the frozen cell
(its control median, 36.81\,ms, matches the frozen 36.77\,ms within
0.1\%). Backward spatial convolution takes 73.0\% of timestamped GPU time
and forward convolution another 16.3\% --- convolution is 89\% of the
step, with three derivative shaders accounting for 28.5\,ms of 36.8
(\code{Conv2dGradWeightGemmSmall} 12.6\,ms over just 12 dispatches marks
the occupancy target). The worst gap in the matrix is a coverage-and-tuning
property of the current convolution-derivative implementations rather than a
uniform graphics-API overhead, and TF32 cuDNN convolutions on the reference
side widen it.

The M3 Whisper profile predates the frozen revision deliberately: it
showed backward attention at 58.3\% of GPU time (\code{FlashGradKV} alone
141.9\,ms of a 330\,ms step). That breakdown motivated the Metal
optimization pass landing in the frozen revision, which cut the cell to
274\,ms --- a $1.20\times$ improvement that moved Apple's worst training
ratio from $3.4\times$ to $2.8\times$. The profile therefore guided an
optimization that survives in the frozen measurements.

The third structural factor is synchronization: the ResNet-50 inference
profile records 177 barrier groups over 198 dispatches, and our companion
cross-vendor study of Blade's pass-boundary barrier model measures
29--32\% GPU gains from eliding redundant compute-pass barriers on
discrete NVIDIA and AMD hardware~\cite{malyshau2026barriers}. Where launch
and synchronization structure dominates, the static plan already wins
(the latency column); where one kernel family dominates, coverage
decides. Each identified gap is an absent specialization or policy ---
no measured gap currently requires an API-level explanation.

\section{The Third P: Productivity and Support}
\label{sec:productivity}

Programmer productivity is not reducible to source-line count; recent P3HPC
work instead measures semantic divergence among implementations
\cite{lin2024productivity}. Our experiment does not contain equivalent ports
in several programming models, nor did it record developer-hours, so we do
not claim such a metric. We report a narrower, operational dimension:
specialization wait, deployment closure, and the support work visible in
standing up one application on the frozen machine set. The 34.5\,KLOC/6.1\,KLOC
implementation size is context for what the measured layer contains, not a
productivity score.

For the footprint comparison, the PyTorch closure follows active
\code{Requires-Dist} edges and requested extras recursively from \code{torch}
and counts each installed file once; the packages-only row sums the
\code{torch} and \code{triton} distributions. The \system{} entry is the
stripped runner, which embeds the compiler, autodiff, runtime, model builders,
and shader sources while relying on ordinary OS libraries and the installed
GPU driver.

Portability studies in HPC often assume the software stack exists on every
platform; on consumer devices, assembling the reference is part of the
support outcome. Each machine needed a different vendor package channel: an
extra wheel index for CUDA, an extra index plus exact version pins for
ROCm (PyPI's default \code{torch} silently resolves to the CUDA build
otherwise), and a full index replacement for Intel's \code{+xpu} build.
The resulting PyTorch versions span 2.10--2.13 because each vendor channel
lags differently. The outcomes differ too: the Intel wheel installs
cleanly yet exposes no usable device; the 780M needed a firmware payload
and the newest ROCm channel, whose PyTorch Whisper backward record fails
the oracle-consistency check (Section~\ref{sec:oracle});
\code{torch.compile} functioned only on the
CUDA and ROCm machines. The portable side of the same experiment is one
native binary per operating system plus the installed graphics
driver --- no per-vendor package source, version pin, or compute runtime
on any machine. These are reproducible configuration facts, not a timed
measure of installation effort.

Two further productivity numbers. Compilation: \system{} prepares a
workload (all three sessions, pipelines included) in 0.08--1.5\,s strict
and at most 2.4\,s with cooperative variants, on every device; where
\code{torch.compile} functions it takes 6.5--96\,s, the maximum being
SmolLM on ROCm (96\,s versus 0.47\,s) --- sub-second specialization is
what makes compile-on-device deployment plausible. Footprint: the
standalone \inferena{} executable is a 12.8\,MiB stripped binary embedding
compiler, autodiff, runtime, model builders, and shaders, against a
1.75--4.66\,GiB Python-side closure (Table~\ref{tab:footprint}). This is a
deployment-closure comparison rather than a feature-equivalence or
developer-productivity claim; Section~\ref{sec:edge} supplies the physical
application check.

\section{Related Work}

Pennycook et al.\ define and later revisit the portability metric and
platform-set discipline used here
\cite{pennycook2016metric,pennycook2021revisiting}. Kokkos and RAJA encode
portable parallelism and memory policy in C++ libraries
\cite{edwards2014kokkos,beckingsale2019raja}; SYCL standardizes a
single-source heterogeneous C++ model~\cite{khronos2020sycl}. This study
instead fixes graphics APIs as the execution substrate and asks what a
domain compiler can recover. Lin et al.'s semantic-divergence metric also
motivates our restraint around the word productivity: our operational
measurements are not a substitute for their programmer-facing method
\cite{lin2024productivity}.

TVM, Glow, MLIR, and Triton established graph and kernel compilation for ML
\cite{chen2018tvm,rotem2018glow,lattner2021mlir,tillet2019triton}; RAF
extends whole-graph compilation to training~\cite{yu2023raf}. Burn~0.21 is a
broader Rust analogue, not a like-for-like compactness baseline: its documented
GPU targets span CUDA, ROCm, Metal, Vulkan, WebGPU, and LibTorch, with CubeCL,
Flex, and LibTorch CPU paths and decorators for autodiff, fusion, routing, and
remote execution~\cite{simard2026burn}. TensorFlow.js demonstrated
browser-resident training and inference~\cite{smilkov2019tensorflowjs}, and
systems for memory-constrained on-device training study a complementary
resource regime~\cite{lin2022ondevice}. Portable Rust autodiff is not the
novelty claimed here. The contribution is a frozen, validity-gated comparison
of one compact native graphics path --- including its complete 12.8\,MiB
runner --- across discrete and integrated devices, coupled to profiles and
deployment evidence.

TinyIREE treats artifact and runtime footprint as first-class deployment
results for embedded inference~\cite{liu2022tinyiree}; ExecuTorch deploys
exported PyTorch programs across mobile backends at scale
\cite{nachin2026executorch}. WebLLM and LlamaWeb demonstrate browser
graphics-API inference performance
\cite{ruan2024webllm,levine2026llamaweb}. \system{} is narrower in framework
breadth, but carries the backward pass, optimizer, and checkpoint through a
native Vulkan/Metal stack. The companion technical report gives the full
system and compiler account~\cite{malyshau2026meganeura}; the Blade study
isolates synchronization in the underlying graphics abstraction
\cite{malyshau2026barriers}.

\section{Threats to Validity}

Five workloads do not establish operator or model coverage; the diffusion
graph is a reduced U-Net, Whisper omits its decoder, and ResNet-50 uses
inference-folded normalization. PyTorch is a moving reference: versions
span 2.10--2.13 by vendor-channel availability, and the eager-only Apple
and Intel references make those ratios generous to \system{} --- though
the conclusion least favorable to us on Apple (training
$1.20$--$2.84\times$ behind) survives the asymmetry, since the handicapped
reference still wins. The Intel CPU reference shows up to
$\sim$1.5$\times$ swings between arithmetic modes whose switches are
no-ops on CPU; Intel ratios are coarse. One machine per vendor and class
(AMD excepted) limits vendor-wide generalization. Strict f32 controls
permissions, not operation order or bitwise identity; gradient-norm gates
can miss elementwise cancellation.

The oracle-consistency rule was applied after inspecting the frozen records,
not preregistered. It removes the same 780M--Whisper backward observation
from both implementations, leaves all raw artifacts and forward results in
place, and makes that one workload's training score a four-machine rather
than five-machine measure. To expose the resulting analytic choice, we also
report the prior one-way assignment as a sensitivity: it changes
\system{}'s mean training score from 0.62 to 0.52 and leaves PyTorch's
rounded mean at 0.93.

The frozen protocol does not add manual CUDA Graph capture. A
purpose-tuned PyTorch deployment could therefore improve the
launch-sensitive minimal-shape cells; this study compares the ordinary
compiled path against \system{}'s automatic static plan, not every possible
application-specific reference optimization.

The matrix reports 20 synchronized samples after warmup from one frozen
process-level run per cell, not independent process replicates; raw samples
expose dispersion but do not justify population-level confidence intervals.
The Pennycook scores are comparative between two implementations and use
the best valid observation between them, not an architecture-normalized
roofline or globally best-known implementation. The gap profiles were
captured one revision before the freeze; the NVIDIA profile's control median
matches the frozen cell within 0.1\%, while the M3 profile is reported
explicitly as the pre-optimization breakdown. Development and tuning time was
not recorded by platform, and substantial bring-up and profile-guided work
occurred on AMD and Apple as well as NVIDIA. The matrix therefore cannot
separate architectural portability from an uneven, evolving history of manual
tuning. Finally, the operational productivity evidence
does not measure developer time, learnability, maintenance cost, or debugging
quality; it supports only the narrower definition stated in the Introduction.

\section{Conclusion}

On the frozen five-machine set, consumer graphics APIs support a credible
ML performance-portability layer, but not a universal performance win.
Under strict f32, the compact stack reaches or beats compiled ROCm PyTorch
on most measured workloads on both AMD devices and wins 12 of 20
GPU-referenced minimal-shape cells, while its median valid training time of
$1.8\times$ PyTorch's and Pennycook training score of 0.62 versus 0.93 show
where mature vendor libraries remain ahead. Profiles turn the largest deficits
into concrete work on convolution derivatives, Metal attention backward,
barrier policy, and cooperative-matrix selection. No measured gap requires
an API-level explanation, but broader workloads could expose one.
Optimization remains ongoing, leaving the NVIDIA ceiling open: how closely a
portable graphics-API stack can approach tuned CUDA/cuBLAS/cuDNN remains
unknown.

Portability is also a support and validity result. One native stack runs on
all five graphics paths, including the Intel machine with no usable vendor
ML GPU path; it compiles models in seconds, closes into a 13\,MiB runner,
and executes inside a physical Quest/OpenXR application. Independent
forward/backward gates and a cross-backend oracle check prevent performance
aggregation from laundering one disagreement: the PyTorch/ROCm 780M Whisper
backward record is inconsistent with the four other PyTorch backends, so the
pair is retained as a diagnostic and excluded symmetrically. Taken together,
the results establish a reproducible baseline
for treating performance, numerical validity, deployment closure, and
platform support as one P3 problem.

\section*{Artifact Description}

\textbf{Availability.} \system{} and \inferena{} are public repositories
(\href{https://github.com/kvark/meganeura}{Meganeura},
\href{https://github.com/kvark/inferena}{Inferena}). Every table in this paper
derives from the revision pair \system{} \code{7561a64} / \inferena{}
\code{7ca9c5c7}, recorded in each of the 50 result artifacts. Both
revisions are preserved under the public tag \code{paper-arxiv-1}; the raw
result bundle accompanies this submission.

\textbf{Contents.} Raw JSON per cell: 20 timing samples per series,
validation diagnostics (output L2, loss error, total and per-parameter
gradient norms), memory figures with explicit basis labels, environment
metadata (GPU, driver, OS, toolchain, precision switches), and the
protocol identifier \code{inferena-paper-v1}. Per-dispatch profile
sidecars record pipeline, workgroup geometry, family, and hardware
timestamps, with instrumentation-overhead ratios. A single script
regenerates every numeric table from these files; a second script asserts
every load-bearing prose number against them.

\textbf{Hardware/software.} Five machines as in Table~\ref{tab:devices}:
RTX~5070 (driver 595.71.05, CUDA 13.0, torch 2.13.0+cu130), RX~7900~XT
(Mesa RADV 26.0.3, ROCm 7.1, torch 2.10.0+rocm7.1), Radeon 780M (Mesa
RADV 25.2.8, ROCm 7.14, torch 2.12.0+rocm7.14.0), Intel RPL-U iGPU (Mesa
ANV 26.0.3, torch 2.11.0+xpu on CPU), Apple M3 (macOS 15.7.3, torch
2.11.0 on MPS). \system{} runs on the installed graphics driver on every
machine; no vendor compute runtime is used.

\textbf{Reproduction.} At the frozen \inferena{} revision, \code{run.sh}
executes the practical contract and \code{--strict} executes the control;
\code{--profile} captures per-dispatch sidecars. The vendor requirements
files are installation starting points rather than lockfiles: in particular,
the two AMD machines use different recorded ROCm channels. Every result
records its exact Python, PyTorch, driver/runtime, precision, and protocol
metadata. Weights are generated deterministically or fetched from
HuggingFace; inputs are deterministic. One full strict-plus-accelerated
sweep takes under an hour per machine, dominated by \code{torch.compile}.

\textbf{Evaluation status.} The artifact was not independently evaluated
before submission. I regenerated every numeric table from the raw
files, rebuilt the paper from a clean auxiliary state, and checked the
load-bearing prose values against the artifacts. DinoVision's
\href{https://github.com/kvark/dinovision}{source} and
\href{https://huggingface.co/mad-bot/dinovision}{evidence archive} are
separately public; those records carry their own source revisions and are not
part of the frozen 50-cell matrix.
