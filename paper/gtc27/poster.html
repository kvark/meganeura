Training and Inference on NVIDIA GPUs Through Vulkan, Measured Against CUDA PyTorch

Dzmitry Malyshau · Independent Researcher · kvark@fastmail.com · github.com/kvark/meganeura

Result. Through the Vulkan driver alone, strict-f32 inference takes 1.0–1.3× CUDA PyTorch's time and training 1.5–1.8×, on three NVIDIA GPU generations under Windows and Linux. The gap widens at reduced precision; single-operation probes locate it.

1Problem and goal

On NVIDIA GPUs, machine learning runs on CUDA and its libraries. The same GPUs also run Vulkan [2], a graphics and compute API that ships inside the driver on Windows and Linux and reaches tensor cores through cooperative-matrix extensions.

Goal: measure how close a compact engine, Meganeura, gets to CUDA PyTorch [1] for training and inference through Vulkan alone, then locate what remains of the gap.

3Benchmarking method

Reference: PyTorch 2.13.0+cu130, torch.compile, CUDA graph replay. One open-source harness runs both engines in fresh, alternating processes.

Strict: f32 throughout, TF32 off. Reduced: PyTorch autocast BF16; Meganeura searches F16/BF16 products and keeps parameters, loss and accumulation in f32.

Data: deterministic inputs; public SmolLM2 checkpoints, synthetic weights elsewhere.

Phases: inference is a full-shape forward; training is forward, loss and backward, without an optimizer update. A minimal-shape forward (one LLM token, no key/value cache) is summarized under the grid.

Gates: Meganeura checks full outputs and single-step gradients against an f32 build. PyTorch checks its forward against eager f32 and its training against an SGD trajectory. Across engines, sampled outputs, loss and gradient norms must agree.

Timing: 10 s warmup, 20 synchronized samples, 3 process pairs; median of process medians.

Metric: Meganeura time ÷ PyTorch time. Below 1, Meganeura is faster.

6Outcomes and next steps

Outcome: all 108 result cells pass on three GPU generations and two operating systems, without CUDA.

Probes became fixes: split-K took a small GEMM from 4.4× to 1.9× cuBLAS's time; a 128×64 tile took 4096³ from 2.1× to 1.7×.

Next, persistent megakernels (in progress) [5]: one launch runs a region of the graph and the GPU schedules dependent tiles, to cut submissions and barriers.

Kernel discovery: let the search compose kernels from tile, staging and fusion choices. Fused attention for long sequences, where Whisper trails most.

Model results: October 31 – November 1, 2026, with the RTX 3050 collected November 10 (Meganeura 567028b8, PyTorch 2.13.0): 102 qualified ratios on these three GPUs (87 core, 15 larger SmolLM2). Single operations: November 8, 2026, Meganeura d32a495; they locate losses and do not partition model time. Protocol, archives and offline replay: [6].
