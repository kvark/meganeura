Training and Inference on NVIDIA GPUs Through Vulkan: Where the Gap to CUDA Lies

Dzmitry Malyshau · Independent Researcher · kvark@fastmail.com · github.com/kvark/meganeura

A 19 MiB Rust engine trains and runs models on NVIDIA GPUs through the Vulkan driver alone. Strict-f32 inference matches CUDA PyTorch on RTX 5070; the gap is in training, reduced precision and a few kernels.

How we measured

Reference: PyTorch 2.13, torch.compile, CUDA graph replay. Strict: f32 throughout, TF32 off. Reduced: PyTorch autocast BF16; Meganeura searches F16/BF16 products, with f32 parameters, loss and accumulation.

Workloads: SmolLM2-135M (public checkpoint), SmolVLA action expert, scaled 10.9M-parameter SD 1.x U-Net, ResNet-50 (folded normalization), Whisper-tiny encoder; all but SmolLM2 use synthetic weights. Phases: batch or 128-token prefill; one token without a KV cache (not decode latency); forward, loss and backward without an optimizer update.

Gates: Meganeura checks full outputs and single-step gradients against an f32 build; PyTorch checks its forward against eager f32 and its training against an SGD trajectory; across engines, sampled outputs, loss and gradient norms must agree. Timing: 10 s warmup, 20 samples, 3 processes.

Driver finding

Driver 595 reads zeros from a dynamically indexed nested local array (array<array<f32,4>,8>, rows 4–7). Flat accumulators avoid it.

3Where the time goes: RTX 5070

  • f32 GEMM: 1.3–1.7× cuBLAS. Both run on CUDA cores; 11.8 vs 19.9 TFLOP/s at 4096³.
  • Split-K: long-K GEMMs went from 4.4× to 1.9×.
  • Winograd 3×3: 0.34–0.64× of cuDNN's batch-1 time.
  • Dispatch ≈ CUDA graph: 0.97 vs 0.85 µs per dependent op.
  • Submit + wait: 19 vs 7 µs per host round trip.
  • Tensor cores: not yet isolated. No TF32 inputs or Hopper warpgroup MMA from Vulkan; their cost needs kernel profiles.
